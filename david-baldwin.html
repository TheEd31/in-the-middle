The David Baldwin Writing Contest

By Leo Wang and Ellen Le

Every year in the middle of January, the David Baldwin Contest begins. Large banners will go up, it'll be written on the Middle School Bulletin, and there will even be an announcement in the Chapel to make sure no one misses it. It's a great experience, and the contest allows students to freely express themselves through creative writing, with there being no requirements in terms of topic—meaning you can write about literally anything you want (as long as it's school-appropriate, of course.) You can write fiction, poetry, essays, and even personal stories.

Every year, many students make submissions, and these submissions are then meticulously critiqued and selected by your very own English Department to determine the winner of the David Baldwin Writing Contest. A winner and an honorable mention will be chosen at the end of reviewing all of the submissions, with the winner earning a spot on a plaque to remember their accomplishment.

This year, the winner was Sara Adibi, and the honorable mention went to Ellen Le (both of whom are, coincidentally, your editors!)

If you haven't entered the contest before, you should! Even if you don't win, there's nothing to lose, and it's actually a great experience to be able to take time to write about whatever you want.
