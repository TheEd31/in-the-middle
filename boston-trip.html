The Sixth-Grade Boston Trip

This year's sixth-grade Boston trip was memorable for every single sixth grader. We learned about everything from the Salem Witch Trials to the Boston Tea Party, while simultaneously making fun memories with friends.

On the first day of the trip, all of the tired but excited sixth graders loaded onto the bus for a ride filled with movies, music, and excitement. Our first stop was at Plymouth Plantation, where we learned about Native American tribes and how they lived back in the 16th and 17th centuries. We then spoke with some actors who were pretending to be from the 13 Colonies, and they answered many questions. After that, we got back on the bus and headed off to Salem. Everyone couldn't wait because we were the first group to go to Salem. Once we got there, we split up into our walking groups and walked to the Salem Witch Trials memorial, where our tour guides told us all about the witch trials, which was one of the things that we saw in Boston and hadn't learned about in class. Once we got to the hotel, everyone went to sleep, excited for the next day.

Day Two: Boston and the Freedom Trail

On the second day, the sixth graders woke up ready for a fun day. The day started with breakfast and then hopping on the bus to drive into Boston. Once we got there, we met up with our tour guides and started walking on the Freedom Trail. We went to Granary Burying Ground, where we saw Paul Revere's grave. Then we went and saw the site of the Boston Massacre. After that, we went to Quincy Market, where we got lunch. There were so many different options of food and souvenirs, which is why it was many people's favorite part of the trip.

After a delicious lunch, we got going again and saw a memorial to Paul Revere. Some fun facts we learned about Paul Revere were that he was a huge contributor to the Boston Tea Party, after his famous midnight ride his horse was actually confiscated when the British saw him, and he also designed and built a gunpowder factory. After visiting the memorial, we drove over to another highlight: the Boston Tea Party Museum, where we got on a ship and threw replicas of the Boston Tea Party tea boxes into the Boston Harbor—a thrilling experience for everyone and a major highlight of the trip. After, we got dinner and then went to bed, sad to leave tomorrow.

Overall, this year's Boston trip was fun for everyone because we made memories with friends while learning a lot about the history of our country.
