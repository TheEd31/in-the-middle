Student Council Events

By Sara Adibi, '30

Student Council runs many events throughout the year, and this spring they helped make new students and teachers feel appreciated.

Easter Egg Hunt

Every spring, Student Council members help set up and run the annual Easter egg hunt. 6th, 7th, and 8th grade students all participate, trading in eggs they collect for candy. Each grade also has the chance to find the golden egg, carefully hidden by a Student Council representative. If a student finds a golden egg, they get a chocolate bunny in return. Student Council works hard to get the school ready for the Easter egg hunt, scattering eggs around the school and finding places to hide the golden egg. They also regulate the event and clean up the broken eggs afterwards.

Kickball Tournament

Student Council also hosted a kickball tournament for all students joining EA middle school next year, whether it was rising sixth graders or new 7th or 8th grade students. This event was designed to help new students build connections with Student Council representatives and other incoming students.

Tess McNeely '31, a seventh grade Student Council member, states, "It was helpful in welcoming students because it helped introduce them to other incoming students. It also gave them the chance to meet other new students going into new grades. Hopefully, they all got a chance to talk to other people which will make their transition into EA easier with some new friends already there."

Jameson Graneri '31, another 7th grade Student Council representative, adds on, "The student kickball tournament helped welcome new students to EA Middle School by giving everyone a fun and relaxed way to meet people outside of class. Since students were working together on teams and cheering each other on, it made it easier for new students to start conversations and feel included."

Teacher Appreciation

Lastly, Student Council helped set up two events to demonstrate teacher appreciation. The first was Dress Up Like A Teacher Day, during which students had the opportunity to dress up as any teacher they'd like, copying their signature styles. This helps teachers feel acknowledged by their students and always gives everyone a laugh.

Student Council also ran an event in which they gave students prompts to pass along to teachers—such as "give this to the teacher who works hard each day," "give this to the teacher who makes every class fun," or "give this to the teacher who makes you smile." There were also sillier prompts like "give this to the teacher most likely to take over the world" and "give this to the teacher most likely to survive the zombie apocalypse."

Jameson Graneri states, "The event where we recorded students giving teachers the prompts helped to show the teachers that they were valued and recognized. It showed that students took time to celebrate them in a personal way."
