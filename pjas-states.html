PJAS States

By Ellen Le, '30, and Leo Wang, '30

There was an article in the last edition that detailed everything about the Pennsylvania Junior Academy of Science (PJAS) Regionals and the Middle School STEM Discovery elective. Now, we're here for a follow-up into PJAS States!

Every year, the PJAS State Competition takes place at the main campus of Penn State University in University Park, which is about a 3-hour drive from EA. With the arrival of over 3,000 competitors, teachers, and judges, PJAS States is one of the largest scientific gatherings in the Pennsylvania area, spanning from Middle School all the way through Upper School. There, students can enjoy ice cream at the famously delicious creamery, visit Penn State campus landmarks, converse with fellow students, and even have the option to stay overnight on Sunday night to really soak up the "vibe."

The Competition Format

Following the exact same format as the Regional competition, you are competing against a rubric instead of other participants—so multiple people can get first-place awards if they do well enough. Although the rubric is the same, the people doing the judging will often have higher expectations, so all of the hard work put into Regionals should be continued going into States.

This year at Regionals, Ellen Le, Elise Markle, Robbie Deirmegian, Jackson Rogers, and Leo Wang all earned a first-place award and are eligible to compete at States on May 18. While their peers attend school that Monday, these students will be presenting their best work at PJAS States.

Once again, no matter the results, each participant has put an exceptional amount of effort into perfecting both their experiment and their presentation, and the entire EA community is excited to see what they'll accomplish. Best of luck to those competing this year!
