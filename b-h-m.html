Black History Month

By Sara Adibi ('31).

Black History Month is nationally celebrated during the month of February. Black History Month is a recognition of the contributions of Black people and the struggles they experienced in the past. At EA, there are many special events and services dedicated to recognizing, studying, and celebrating the history, achievements, culture, and contributions of Black people.

Black History Month was first introduced by historian Carter G. Woodson in 1926. It was originally only a week in February. February was selected to coincide with the birthdays of Abraham Lincoln and Frederick Douglass, both of whom were figures that were important in the fight against slavery. After the civil rights movement, activists pushed to expand the week into an entire month. Finally, in 1986, Congress officially recognized and established February as Black History Month.

EA honors Black History Month in various ways, and one of the integral ways of honoring the month is through chapel services. In previous years, a performance group called Key Arts Productions taught students about Black History Month through an interactive presentation in the Chapel.

“I found this to be meaningful because it was engaging and creative, as they had catchy music and their audience loved it!” Middle School History teacher Jerome Bailey states. “In addition, a few years ago we did a door decoration contest which recognized famous Black people in American History.”

This year, an early start to honoring Black History Month was MLK chapel, in which lower school students presented facts and quotes highlighting MLK’s contributions and ideals. Mr. Bailey adds, “I would hope that the student body learns that African Americans have overcome hardships throughout American History and, despite discrimination and unfair treatment, they were still able to persevere and resist to achieve success. I think it is also important to be aware of lesser-known people and their stories. While Martin Luther King and Rosa Parks are important figures, there are many other individuals—not only African Americans—who contributed to racial equality in this country, and their stories are equally important.”

The Middle School Black Student Union (BSU) honored the month through an interactive chapel performance on February 23. Several students were invited to participate in a Family Feud-style trivia game, where all the questions were about famous Black figures and their contributions to society. Black History Month is an annual recognition of the critical role African Americans have played in shaping history and their fight for equality in this country.
