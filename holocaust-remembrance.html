International Holocaust Remembrance Day

By Zach Chen and Victor Tsai ('30, '30)

The Holocaust was a genocide of various minority groups in Nazi Germany. These groups included Jews, people with disabilities, Jehovah's Witnesses, people of color, and LGBTQ members. These groups were sent to concentration camps, such as Terezin, where they were held until they were sent to be executed. There were many execution camps, including, but not limited to, Auschwitz.

International Holocaust Remembrance Day is a way for newer generations to learn about what happened. More importantly, we must recognize the critical lessons of Holocaust history as we commemorate the victims and honor the survivors. January 27th marks International Holocaust Remembrance Day, chosen because it is the anniversary of the liberation of Auschwitz-Birkenau in 1945. Established by the United Nations in 2005, the day serves as a global reminder of what state-sponsored hatred can become when left unchecked.

Over six million Jews were killed during the Holocaust, alongside hundreds of thousands of others. These were not statistics. They were students, teachers, parents, and children.

The Holocaust may feel like distant history, but its lessons are anything but. Antisemitism, discrimination, and the dehumanization of minority groups remain real problems around the world today. Remembrance is not just about looking back, but about making sure nothing like this ever happens again.
