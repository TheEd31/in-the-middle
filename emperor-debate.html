The Emperor Debate

By Victor Tsai, '30, and Leo Wang, '30

Who's the best Roman emperor? If you're not in 8th grade, you probably have no clue or you don't really care. But for the Class of 2030, this question has been the backbone of Latin class the entire year. The 8th grade Latin X contest is a (not-so) friendly year-long competition in which multiple teams from each Latin class are each assigned a different Roman emperor. It's all luck-based too, since emperors are chosen through a raffle, meaning you could have first-pick, or be left with someone like Commodus. Then, throughout the year, teams research, defend, and absolutely glaze the living daylights out of their emperor—whether or not they think they're even good—in order to earn some points for their power rankings.

How It Works

There are multiple major events to earn MASSIVE points and climb the ladder to hopefully have the most points at the end of the year, like the Argumentum Videos, Evening with the Emperors, or the grand finale: The Emperor Debates. Starting with the class rounds, each class holds debates between the emperors within the class. Each team attempts to answer questions regarding their emperor's achievements and why they should be brought back from the Underworld. Then, when both sides have answered their respective questions, they are let off the leash and finally have the ability to criticize the opposing emperor and formulate rebuttals. The winner of the class debate is nominated to then head to the big stage, where the OFFICIAL EMPEROR DEBATE will occur.

Then, the top performers from each class will go head to head, battling it out to prove once and for all which Roman emperor is the best of them all. The winner of the debate has the potential to climb quite a lot of the ladder, meaning those in first might not stay in first for long, which makes the debate super high stakes.

At the end of the day, though, the entire goal of this year-long competition is not to fester hatred (although it definitely did), but rather to allow students to study both sides of ancient Rome's culture; the good, the bad, and the ugly. Good luck to all of the teams competing in this final debate!
