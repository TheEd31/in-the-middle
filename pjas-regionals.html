PJAS Regionals

By Leo Wang, '30, and Ellen Le, '30

Electives are taken in both your 7th-grade and 8th-grade years, allowing you to branch out and indulge in exemplary experiences. The range is also quite diverse, going from fun ones like World According to Board Games and D&D all the way to more STEM-oriented ones like Marine Biology and Astronomy & Astrophysics. But the most prestigious of them all is likely the STEM Discovery elective.

For the students who want to attend this elective, getting into it is actually quite difficult. The people who manage to get in have to be selected, meaning that there will be people who, unfortunately, will not be allowed to take it. However, even for the ones who get in, it's not an easy course. Spanning only the time of a trimester, students will work towards the PJAS (Pennsylvania Junior Academy of Science) competition. They will have to design an experiment following a type of STEM subject—any field of science (biology, chemistry, physics, etc.), technology, engineering, or even mathematics. Meticulously accounting for every variable and constant in their environments, students will then perform experiments, collect data, and create a presentation to show to judges. Unlike a normal competition, though, you are graded against a rubric—meaning, multiple people can get first-place prizes if they do well enough. So, it's completely reliant on how much effort you put in as an individual.

As students collect the results from their experiment, they organize them into tables and graphs that are then put into their presentations to detail everything about their experiment. From variables and materials to sources of error and background research, the presentation slides are the culmination of the information gathered by the students, and they're what will be shown to judges. Students who earned first awards will have the opportunity to advance to a state-level competition and, once again, present their work.

This Year's Competition

This year, the people in this elective were Ryan Burke, Helmut Dang, Robbie Deirmengian, Jack Kuhnmunch, Ellen Le, Jake Liang, Elise Markle, Jackson Rogers, Tommy van Binsbergen, and Leo Wang. Each student conducted their own unique experiment, and then, with their completed presentation, traveled to E.T. Richardson Middle School on February 28 to give their presentations.

This year at the Regional competition, Ellen Le, Elise Markle, Robbie Deirmengian, Jackson Rogers, and Leo Wang all earned a first-place award and will be advancing to the State Competition later in May.

In the end, no matter the results, each student has put an exceptional amount of effort into perfecting their experiment, and the encounters and knowledge gained from their research are the real fruit that is borne from this elective. So, whether it's your first time in 7th grade or your 2nd round in 8th, it's highly recommended you give this fun yet challenging elective a try.
